# What is it
Separate advocacy is when two sides propose their own point of view without any substantial challenge. With no presented counterargument it becomes difficult to effectively appraise each view, particularly given a prior opinion. This is a pitfall that both a person trying to reach a neutral perspective by looking at both sides, and an organisation trying to present a neutral perspective, like the [[FalseBalance-BMJ.pdf|BBC]], must do their best to avoid.

## The Backfire effect
Research has shown that presenting someone with an opposing opinion actually *reinforces* the pre-existing opinion, known as the [[nyhan-reifler.pdf|backfire effect]].
Furthermore there is evidence to suggest that [[Exposure to opposing views on social media can increase political polarization-2 1.pdf|exposure to opposing views can increase political polarisation]], because the brain shifts from a critical thinking mode to one of [[Emotive Reasoning|identity protection]]. This is closely linked with the [boomerang effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomerang_effect_(psychology)) where "*an attempt at persuasion can result in the adoption of an opposing opinion"*.

# V.S. the BBC
The BBC can often suffer a similar [[FalseBalance-BMJ.pdf|pitfall]], however as the news content is not opinionated and as such is still a presentation of facts, the effect of separate advocacy is diminished compared with that of a person reading opinion columns from both sides. I believe [[FalseBalance-BMJ.pdf|this paper]] applies not just to the BBC but to anyone who tries to collate a neutral range of media.
