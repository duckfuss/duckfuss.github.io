## **Current drug discovery**
- Process can take more than a decade
- Can take billions of dollars in expenditure before a molecule can be recognised as a drug
> - “A significant portion” of this time and money is invested in identifying molecules that might exhibit “significant medicinal activity agains a disease, usually referred to as a *hit”*
> - Most research focuses on hits of low molecular weight (<900 daltons, or 1nm or less), which constitute around 78% of the drug market
- The medicinal activity of a drug candidate (a.k.a a ligand) is given by its ability to bind to a biological *target* (e.g. a protein), which regulates the metabolism of the disease
**Stages in typical drug discovery**
1. Generate library of potential drug candidates
2. Screen based on medicinal activity to identify hits
3. Screen further based on factors like efficacy and potency of hits
4. Optimise into candidates called *lead compounds*
5. Proceed through stages of drug development and clinical trials till one lead becomes a viable commercial product
### **High-throughput screening (HTS)**
- Screening of large molecular library using *in vitro* activity experiments
- Low hit rates
- Require synthesis of large no. compounds
	- Mean significant investment of resources ant time is required  
- Since the 70s computational chemistry and statistical analysis have sped up HTS
	- Higher hit rate
	- Increase in quality of leads
	- Resulted in practice of *computer aided drug design (CADD)*
### **Computer Aided Drug Design (CADD)**
- Aim of CADD is to answer the question:
> 	- *What are the best chemical structures associated with a desired therapeutic effect?*
- 2 main challenges with answering this question
	1. Accuracy of simulation of interactions between candidate drugs and biological targets
		- limited by computational limits
	1. Accuracy of statistical modelling of activities and ADMET profiles (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity)
		- Limited by modern statistical techniques

## Quantum Computer advantage
- QCs can use entanglement to perform operations impossible on a classical computer 
- QCs are great at efficient manipulation of linear algebraic operations
- Quantum devices with just more than 50 qubits would make it possible to map problems which are beyond classical computers
