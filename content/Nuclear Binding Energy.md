# summary
- Both the experimental and theoretical views are equivalent, with slightly different emphasis on what the binding energy means.
## Experimental physics definition 
- minimum energy required to disassemble the nucleus of an atom into its constituent [[nucleons|nucleons]] (experimental physics definition)
- [[nucleons|nucleons]]  are attracted to each other by the [[strong nuclear force|strong nuclear force]] 
## Theoretical physics definition 
- represents the energy of a nucleus _relative_ to the energy of its constituent parts
# calculated
- when the nucleus was formed (via [[nuclear fusion|nuclear fusion]]?) some energy (converted FROM Mass (remember [[energy-mass equivalence|E=mc^2]])) was released. The missing mass is known as the ***mass defect***
- [[energy-mass equivalence|E = mc^2]] where:
	- E = [[Nuclear Binding Energy|Nuclear Binding Energy]] 
	- m = mass defect 
	- c = speed of light
# [[nuclear fusion|Fusion]] & [[NuclearFission|Fission]]:
- in order for [[nuclear fusion|nuclear fusion]] and [[NuclearFission|NuclearFission]] to produce energy, the [[Nuclear Binding Energy|Nuclear Binding Energy]] of the resultant elements must be greater than those of the starting elements
