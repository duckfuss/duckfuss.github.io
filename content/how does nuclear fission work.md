# how does it work?
- related to[[nuclear fusion#How it works:|fusion, how does it work]]
## the Setup
- 2 opposing forces:
	- the [[Coulomb force|Coulomb force]] and the [[strong nuclear force|strong nuclear force]](manifested as the **[[nuclear force|nuclear force]]**)
- the [[Coulomb force|Coulomb force]] causes oppositely charged protons to repel
- [[strong nuclear force|strong nuclear force]] holds protons and neutrons together in the atomic nucleus
## why does it only work for elements > Fe?
- elements under Fe require net INPUT of energy in order to overcome the [[nuclear force|nuclear force]]
	- this is because the [[nuclear force|nuclear force]] only acts in very small scales and the [[Coulomb force|Coulomb force]] acts infinitely, overpowering the [[nuclear force|nuclear force]] in not tiny distances
	- nuclei lighter than Fe experience the [[nuclear force|nuclear force]] more greatly, for they are smaller and sufficiently proton poor.
	- in heavier nuclei the [[nuclear force|nuclear force]] is too small scale to act across all [[nucleons|nucleons]]
- beyond iron, the binding energy drops as the influence of the [[nuclear force|nuclear force]] diminishes

## What happens
- the fission splits more unstable nuclei into smaller nuclei with higher binding energies, thus converting mass into energy ([[energy-mass equivalence|E = mc^2]])
